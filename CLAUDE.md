# Postcard City Development Guide

## Project intent
Postcard City is a high-quality political regional city simulator. The game must make urban transformation legible, politically consequential, and emotionally memorable without becoming an opaque spreadsheet.

## Quality bar
Design, narrative, accessibility, technical stability, and presentation should be reviewed against a premium award-level target. A feature is not complete merely because it works; it must be understandable, testable, accessible, and valuable to the core experience.

## Non-negotiable principles
1. Protect the core fantasy: govern a living region under pressure.
2. Prefer systemic consequences over moral labels.
3. Make cause and effect inspectable.
4. Do not make tourism automatically good or bad.
5. Do not make corruption a binary toggle; treat it as power gained through institutional debt.
6. Keep residents, workers, institutions, businesses, media, and opposition meaningfully distinct.
7. Use authored scenario arcs around procedural variation; never rely on noise alone.
8. Support scalable complexity without deleting the core experience.
9. Build localization and accessibility into the architecture from the beginning.
10. Every major system needs deterministic tests and a player-facing explanation.

## Working rules
- Keep design documents in English.
- Use explicit assumptions and versioned decisions.
- Separate domain rules from UI and presentation.
- Prefer data-driven content.
- Do not expand scope without identifying what is removed, deferred, or simplified.
- Validate procedural scenarios for plausibility, solvability, and meaningful choice.
- Treat performance, save reliability, and deterministic seeds as first-class features.

## Definition of done
A feature is done when its design intent, data contract, implementation, tests, accessibility behavior, localization behavior, and failure modes are documented and verified.
