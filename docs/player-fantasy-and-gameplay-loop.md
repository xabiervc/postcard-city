# Player Fantasy and Gameplay Loop

## Player fantasy

The player is not an omnipotent mayor. The player is a civic institution trying to steer a living city under pressure while negotiating with groups whose interests conflict.

The core fantasy is:

> Decide what kind of city deserves to be remembered, and accept the costs required to preserve it.

The player can:

- set priorities;
- select and sequence interventions;
- allocate scarce public resources;
- negotiate with affected groups;
- accept uncertainty and delayed consequences;
- document what changed and why.

The player cannot:

- directly control every resident;
- remove all trade-offs;
- guarantee that an intervention works;
- prevent every crisis;
- treat metrics as a substitute for lived consequences.

## Minute-to-minute loop

```text
observe -> interpret -> prioritize -> intervene -> resolve -> remember
```

| Phase | Player action | Information | Cost | Feedback | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Observe | Inspect districts, indicators, testimonies, and prior postcards | Mixed quantitative and qualitative evidence | Attention/time | Map, report, voices | Detects pressure or opportunity |
| Interpret | Compare causes, mechanisms, and competing explanations | Evidence quality and uncertainty | Cognitive load | Causal preview | Forms a hypothesis |
| Prioritize | Choose one problem or value to protect | Budget, political support, relationships | Opportunity cost | Priority marker | Fixes the next objective |
| Intervene | Apply a decision, mechanism, land action, or housing action | Requirements and forecast range | Budget, legitimacy, time | Immediate state change and trace | Changes the city |
| Resolve | Advance the simulation and handle a crisis | Immediate and delayed effects | Irreversible time | Events, metrics, testimonies | Produces consequences |
| Remember | Create or compare a postcard | Current and historical state | Archive slot/attention | Before/after contrast | Gives meaning to change |

## Session contract

A short vertical-slice session lasts 20–30 minutes and contains:

- one district under visible pressure;
- three to four recurring characters or perspectives;
- two meaningful interventions;
- at least one delayed consequence;
- one crisis requiring a priority choice;
- one initial and one later postcard;
- a causal summary explaining the major change;
- an explicit success condition and an explicit failure condition.

## Design constraints

- A player should make no more than three high-consequence decisions per cycle.
- Every major metric must have at least one visible, social, or material representation.
- Every irreversible action must show its main known trade-off before confirmation.
- Delayed consequences must become legible through traces, events, testimony, or changed postcards.
- The game must communicate uncertainty as a range or confidence level, not false precision.

## Prototype-only questions

The following cannot be settled by documentation alone:

- whether the information density is enjoyable;
- whether the player understands causality without external explanation;
- whether the emotional impact is sufficient;
- whether a cycle feels too slow or too fast;
- whether the postcard comparison is memorable.

These require a playable vertical slice and structured playtests.
