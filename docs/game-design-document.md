# Game Design Document

## 1. Role and objective
The player is the elected regional or metropolitan governor. The role has authority over regional planning, housing policy, transport, public services, economic development, tourism regulation, public communication, and selected appointments.

The objective is not a single moral score. The player must maintain governability across multiple mandates while preserving enough economic capacity, public services, housing access, legitimacy, and resilience for the region to function.

## 2. Core loop
1. Inspect the region dashboard and local reports.
2. Identify emerging risks and opportunities.
3. Consult residents, institutions, businesses, media, and opposition.
4. Choose policy, investment, regulation, or communication.
5. Advance time through a simulation tick.
6. Observe economic, spatial, social, political, and visual effects.
7. Respond to events, investigations, protests, and elections.
8. Review the mandate and adjust the strategy.

## 3. Core resources and outcomes
- Public budget.
- Housing affordability.
- Permanent population stability.
- Worker accessibility.
- Healthcare capacity.
- Transport reliability.
- Tourism demand and saturation.
- Economic diversity.
- Institutional trust.
- Political support.
- Media credibility environment.
- Regional resilience.

These are not interchangeable scores. Each has causes, thresholds, lag, and distribution across districts.

## 4. Major actors
- Residents and households.
- Essential workers.
- Tourists.
- Local businesses.
- Large investors.
- Public institutions.
- Political parties.
- Opposition groups.
- Journalists and media organizations.
- Civil society and unions.

Actors have interests, resources, information, relationships, and memory. They should react to outcomes and perceived fairness, not only to raw numbers.

## 5. Decisions
Decision types include zoning, housing regulation, public investment, transport, service capacity, tourism policy, economic development, grants, media support, contracts, transparency, and emergency response.

Each decision has visible immediate effects, uncertain or delayed effects, affected groups, budget implications, reversibility, and information requirements.

## 6. Politics
Elections are time-bound evaluations of outcomes, perception, mobilization, and coalition support. The player can govern for residents, private power, personal gain, institutional legitimacy, or an unstable mixture. Political identity emerges from repeated decisions rather than being a fixed morality class.

## 7. Media and scandals
Media organizations have audience, credibility, resources, ownership, editorial independence, and coverage priorities. Potential scandals arise from hidden facts, sources, evidence quality, journalist capacity, verification, publication, and government response. Public funding can support pluralism or become a tool of capture depending on its design and administration.

## 8. Economic opportunities
Projects can be beneficial, harmful, speculative, predatory, or fraudulent. Their public value differs from private profit. Proposals contain claims, conditions, infrastructure demands, legal requests, labor needs, uncertainty, and possible hidden liabilities.

## 9. City transformation
The region visibly transitions between local, mixed, tourist-oriented, branded, and hollowed-out states. Visual changes must correspond to simulation state: housing use, business mix, signage, public space, traffic, residents, and service activity.

## 10. Victory and failure
Campaign and scenario outcomes are multidimensional. Possible outcomes include resilient diversification, balanced destination, tourist enclave, regional fragmentation, managed shrinkage, institutional capture, bankruptcy, service collapse, or electoral defeat.

Failure should usually result from functional or political collapse, not from a single moral metric. Sandbox mode may continue after electoral defeat or major setbacks depending on configuration.

## 11. Complexity modes
- Casual: summarized systems and clear recommendations.
- Standard: core social, economic, and political systems.
- Advanced: media, corruption, uncertainty, contracts, and deeper factions.
- Custom: per-system toggles and resolution levels.

Reducing complexity should aggregate systems, not remove the central fantasy.
