# Procedural Generation Bible

## Goal
Generate coherent regional cases with different problems and opportunities, not arbitrary random cities.

## Generation layers
1. Geography template.
2. Urban hierarchy.
3. Development history.
4. Demographics and housing.
5. Economic base.
6. Infrastructure and services.
7. Political and media landscape.
8. Primary arc.
9. Secondary arcs.
10. Opportunities and crisis schedule.

## Geography templates
- Coastal historic city.
- Inland provincial capital.
- Port and industrial city.
- Metropolitan commuter belt.
- New planned city.
- Shrinking industrial region.
- University city.
- Border region.

## Primary arcs
- Tourism dependency.
- Housing emergency.
- Industrial transition.
- Shrinking population.
- Infrastructure deficit.
- Institutional capture.
- Megaproject gamble.
- Security and social cohesion crisis.

## Compatibility rules
Templates define valid combinations and parameter ranges. A new city must have at least one viable recovery route, at least two meaningful strategies, and no unavoidable failure in the first evaluation horizon.

## Opportunity generation
Opportunities are selected from context-aware pools. A high-quality industrial proposal requires appropriate workforce, infrastructure, land, governance reliability, and housing capacity. A distressed region may still receive proposals, but they should reflect its conditions and may be lower quality or more extractive.

## Crisis generation
Crises are triggered by state, history, actors, and season. Random selection is weighted by causal context. A crisis must have warning signals, response options, and recoverable as well as damaging outcomes.

## Validation
Generated scenarios are checked for:
- Structural validity.
- Reachability of required services.
- Budget solvency horizon.
- At least two viable strategies.
- No contradictory tags.
- Appropriate difficulty.
- Explainable initial diagnosis.
- Seed reproducibility.

## Replayability
Replayability comes from different causal situations, actor relationships, information quality, and event order. Cosmetic map variation is secondary.
