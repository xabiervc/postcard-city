# Campaign Structure

## Dramatic promise

The campaign follows a city trying to remain livable and recognizable while economic pressure, housing scarcity, political conflict, and public emergencies intensify.

## Acts

| Act | Function | Player experience | Required consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. The postcard | Establish the place and its competing values | Observe, interpret, and choose a first priority | The player commits to a direction |
| II. Pressure lines | Expose trade-offs and faction conflict | Intervene while resources tighten | A delayed cost becomes visible |
| III. The crisis | Force a choice between incompatible protections | Resolve an emergency under uncertainty | One value is protected at another's expense |
| IV. The point of no return | Make prior choices constrain the future | Respond to accumulated consequences | A relationship, district, or resource cannot be restored cheaply |
| V. The remembered city | Show the resulting city and its memory | Compare postcards and testimonies | The player sees improvement, loss, and unresolved risk |

## Campaign boundaries

- A short campaign contains 5 acts and 8–12 high-consequence decisions.
- Each act introduces at most one major new system.
- At most two major crises are active simultaneously.
- The campaign must reach a readable resolution rather than continue indefinitely.
- Replay variation comes from priorities, context, relationships, and delayed consequences, not arbitrary randomness.

## Vertical-slice campaign

The slice covers Acts I–III in 20–30 minutes:

- one district;
- four recurring characters;
- two interventions;
- one crisis;
- one delayed consequence;
- two postcards;
- one success and one failure condition.

## Success and failure

Success means the player preserves a declared priority while keeping the city within minimum livability bounds.

Failure means the declared priority collapses, or the player reaches the crisis with no credible response because earlier trade-offs exhausted the relevant capacity.

Neither outcome is a simple moral score. The final report must explain what was protected, what was sacrificed, and what remains unresolved.
