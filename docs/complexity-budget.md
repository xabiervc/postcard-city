# Complexity Budget

## Purpose

The game should feel like a consequential civic decision space, not an unreadable administrative dashboard. Complexity is budgeted as a player-facing resource.

## Vertical-slice limits

- One district is active on the main screen.
- No more than 12 primary metrics are visible without opening detail.
- No more than 4 recurring characters are active.
- No more than 2 major crises are active simultaneously.
- No more than 3 high-consequence decisions occur in one cycle.
- Each act introduces at most one major system.
- A causal explanation exposes at most 5 dominant links before progressive disclosure.
- A standard resolution should complete within 2 seconds on target hardware.
- A full 20–30 minute slice should not require more than 30 minutes of real-time simulation.

## Full-campaign targets

- 5 acts;
- 8–12 high-consequence decisions;
- 6–8 recurring perspectives available, with at most 4 foregrounded at once;
- at most 2 simultaneous major crises;
- 20–30 primary metrics, with grouping and progressive disclosure;
- one readable summary after every major resolution.

## Escalation rules

When a new system is introduced, one existing presentation layer must be simplified, grouped, or deferred. New complexity must earn its place by enabling a distinct player decision.

## Performance and legibility gates

A change fails review if it:

- adds a metric without a decision attached to it;
- creates a crisis that cannot be explained in one short paragraph;
- requires the player to inspect more than three panels for a routine decision;
- hides an irreversible cost behind a secondary screen;
- makes the main cycle slower without increasing meaningful choice.
